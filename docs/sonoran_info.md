# Sonoran Desert — Info Button Text (game content)

Source region: Arizona Upland Sonoran Desert around Tucson, AZ.
Rainfall basis: NWS Tucson 1991–2020 normals, annual 10.61 in (~270 mm),
bimodal: winter rains Dec–Mar + summer monsoon Jul–Sep, foresummer drought Apr–Jun.
See `data/sonoran_rainfall.json`. __DOI or station lookup via NOAA U.S. Climate Normals.__

## Typical weather year

- **Winter rains (Dec–Mar, ~60–75 mm total):** Gentle Pacific frontal storms.
  Cool (highs 65–76 °F), best soil moisture of the year. Light but reliable.
- **Foresummer drought (Apr–Jun):** Hottest, driest stretch. Highs 83–101 °F,
  almost no rain (~17 mm total). New plantings die without irrigation.
- **Monsoon (Jul–Sep, ~140 mm, over half the annual total):** Intense
  afternoon thunderstorms, flash floods, dust storms, extreme heat (highs 95–100 °F).
  Most effective planting window — heat + water together.
- **Autumn (Oct–Nov):** Drying out, mild temperatures (highs 75–86 °F).
  Good second planting window after monsoon moisture banks in the soil.

## Best time to plant

1. **Monsoon onset (late Jun–Jul)** — top pick for natives like velvet mesquite:
   warm soil + coming rains = fast root growth. Dig basins/swales BEFORE the
   storms so runoff soaks in.
2. **Post-monsoon / fall (Sep–Oct)** — second best: warm soil, milder air,
   stored soil moisture; roots establish over winter.
3. **Late winter (Feb–Mar)** — third: cool-season moisture still in soil;
   plant early enough to root before the Apr–Jun drought.
4. **Avoid Apr–Jun** unless you can irrigate heavily — survival penalty
   (~40% lower establishment, see `data/mesquite.json`).

Rule of thumb: plant with the rains, not against them — monsoon for
heat-lovers, fall/winter for cool-season annuals and perennials.

## Typical problems

- **Foresummer desiccation:** Apr–Jun kills unirrigated seedlings; even mature
  trees stress. Symptom: leaf drop, scorched margins.
- **Monsoon washouts:** Intense cells erode swales/berms, drown seedlings in
  pooling clay, snap weak limbs (microbursts).
- **Caliche & poor infiltration:** Cemented calcium layer blocks roots and
  drainage; water sheets off instead of soaking in.
- **Pests/browsers:** Jackrabbits girdle young trees; bruchid beetles infest
  mesquite pods; mistletoe weakens branches.
- **Extreme heat (100 °F+ ~68 days/yr):** Transplant shock, irrigation
  evaporation losses, dry winds desiccate mulch.
- **Overpumping:** Too many wells / thirsty non-natives crash the water table;
  mesquite taproots compete with wells in the game model.

## Strategies that work here

- **Rainwater harvesting earthworks:** Swales on contour, half-moon berms,
  micro-basins sized to monsoon bursts; mulch pits around each tree.
- **Plant the rain:** Time planting to monsoon/fall; mulch 10 cm deep, keep
  mulch off trunks.
- **Nurse-tree guilds:** Velvet mesquite as nitrogen-fixing overstory —
  fertility island + shade cuts nearby water need ~10%. Underplant with
  tepary beans, squash, prickly pear, cholla.
- **Break caliche:** Rip planting holes through caliche, backfill with native
  soil (not rich potting mix — discourages deep rooting).
- **Protect seedlings:** Tree tubes/cages against rabbits for 2 years;
  deep-infrequent watering to drive taproots down, then taper off.
- **Monsoon-proof structures:** Armor spillways with rock, keep berms low and
  broad, clean silt after big storms.
- **Go native first:** Non-natives cost ~2–3× the water; natives survive the
  foresummer on rainfall + stored soil water once established.

## Velvet mesquite in one line

Fast-growing (~60 cm/yr to ~9 m), deep-rooted nitrogen fixer; pods mill into
flour, canopy nurses crops, but give it room — wide roots and thirsty
seedlings the first two summers.
